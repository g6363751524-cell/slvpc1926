Frequently Asked Questions About Termite Treatment

Clear, factual answers regarding subterranean termite identification, structural damage, inspection-based pricing, preparation, and barrier treatments.

Q01

How do I know if my property has termites?

Key signs include earthen mud shelter tubes extending along walls or door jambs, wooden frames sounding paper-thin or hollow when tapped, discarded swarmer wings on windowsills during monsoon evenings, tight-fitting doors caused by swollen timber, and subtle blistering of wall paint.

Q02

What are the common signs of termites?

Common indicators include: (1) brown mud tubes across masonry surfaces, (2) hollow, brittle woodwork with intact paint veneers, (3) piles of shed translucent wings near exterior openings, (4) fine soil particles plugged into drywall pinholes, and (5) sagging or jammed wooden fixtures.

Q03

Where do termites usually occur?

Subterranean termites originate in ground soil and enter properties through foundation plinth joints, flooring expansion gaps, plumbing wall penetrations, and electrical conduits. Once inside, they typically colonize door frames, skirting boards, built-in wardrobes, modular kitchen cabinetry, and false ceiling timber battens.

Q04

Can termites damage a property?

Yes. Subterranean termites consume cellulose—the primary structural polymer in natural wood, plywood, and paper-faced drywall. Left unaddressed, colonies can hollow out structural door frames, compromise kitchen cabinetry, damage wooden flooring underlay, and chew through electrical wiring insulation within conduits.

Q05

How much does termite treatment cost?

Termite treatment pricing is determined after an on-site property inspection. Because cost depends on the linear footage of masonry to be treated, woodwork volume, construction design, and property layout, an inspection is required to provide an accurate, transparent quotation.

Q06

Why is inspection required?

An on-site inspection allows the technician to confirm whether activity is active or dormant, identify subterranean entry pathways through foundation masonry, assess woodwork damage depth, and formulate the appropriate drill-and-infusion barrier protocol for your specific property layout.

Q07

How long does termite treatment take?

Treatment duration depends on the property dimensions, number of door frames and affected rooms, and linear feet of drilling required. A typical residential apartment treatment usually takes several hours, while large independent villas or multi-story commercial facilities require more time as evaluated during inspection.

Q08

Is treatment suitable for apartments?

Yes. Termites frequently travel up central plumbing stacks and expansion joints into upper-floor apartments. Our drill-and-infusion techniques are specifically calibrated for apartment skirting boards and door frames, using low-noise equipment and aesthetic color-matched hole sealing.

Q09

Can existing termite activity return?

While professional non-repellent chemical barriers establish a resilient protective zone in treated masonry, termites can potentially find new untreated entry cracks if subsequent structural modifications occur or if external soil conditions shift. Following proper post-treatment maintenance and scheduling periodic inspections helps ensure ongoing property defense.

Q10

What preparation is required?

Key preparation steps include moving loose furniture approximately 2 to 3 feet away from walls and skirting boards to provide technician access, clearing items from the base of wardrobes and under-sink cabinets, and identifying areas where previous activity was noticed.

Q11

What should I do after treatment?

Allow treated drilled holes and sealant to dry completely. Avoid washing skirting boards with excessive standing water immediately following service, resolve any persistent plumbing leaks that contribute to structural dampness, and follow the technician’s post-service monitoring guidelines.

Q12

Do you provide follow-up and monitoring?

Yes. SLV Pest Control provides structured follow-up and monitoring guidance according to your specific service agreement terms to evaluate chemical barrier performance and verify that structural activity has been successfully arrested.
